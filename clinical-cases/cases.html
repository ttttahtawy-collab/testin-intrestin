Case 1 Cardiology · 10 marks

A 58-year-old male smoker has had 2 hours of crushing retrosternal pain with sweating. BP 85/50 mmHg, pulse 54/min, JVP raised, lungs clear. ECG: ST elevation in II, III and aVF. The nearest primary-PCI centre is 3 hours away.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Name the likely culprit artery and the extra ECG lead you would request (and why). [2]
  2. Mention two investigations apart from the ECG and what each adds. [1]
  3. Write the immediate drug treatment with doses. [3]
  4. Which reperfusion strategy is appropriate here, and why? List 3 absolute contraindications to it. [2]
  5. Special: Which drugs must be avoided in this patient, and how would you treat his hypotension? [2]

QModel answerMarks
1Inferior STEMI complicated by right-ventricular infarction (hypotension, ↑JVP, clear lungs). Culprit: RCA (~85%; otherwise LCx). Request right-sided leads (V4R): ST↑ in V4R confirms RV infarct (proximal RCA).1+0.5+0.5
2Any two: serial troponin I/T (rises 3–6 h, peak 36–48 h); echo (wall motion, RV dilatation, mechanical complications); CBC, kidney function, electrolytes, lipids. Do not delay reperfusion waiting for results.0.5 each
3
  • Aspirin 162–325 mg chewed, then 75–100 mg daily
  • Clopidogrel 300–600 mg load → 75 mg daily (or Ticagrelor 180 mg → 90 mg bid)
  • Anticoagulant: Enoxaparin 30 mg IV bolus then 1 mg/kg SC q12h (<75 y), or UFH 60 U/kg IV (max 4000) → 12 U/kg/h
  • Atorvastatin 80 mg; O₂ only if hypoxic; Morphine 2–4 mg IV for refractory pain
  • Bradycardia: Atropine 0.5–1 mg IV (max 3 mg). Withhold β-blocker (shock, bradycardia).
0.5 each (max 3)
4Fibrinolysis (PCI is >120 min away): door-to-needle ≤30 min, e.g. Tenecteplase (weight-based) or Alteplase 15 mg → 50 mg/30 min → 35 mg/60 min, or Streptokinase 1.5 MIU/60 min; then transfer for angiography.
Absolute CI (any 3): prior intracranial hemorrhage · cerebral AVM · intracranial malignancy · ischemic stroke <3 months · suspected aortic dissection · active bleeding/bleeding diathesis · significant head/face trauma <3 months.		1 + 1
5RV infarct is preload-dependent: avoid nitrates and diuretics (also PDE-5 inhibitors with nitrates). Treat hypotension with IV normal-saline boluses; inotropes if no response.1 + 1
Pearl: Inferior STEMI + hypotension + clear lungs = RV infarct until proven otherwise. Giving nitroglycerin here can crash the BP.

Case 2 Cardiology · 10 marks

A 12-year-old girl (35 kg) from rural Upper Egypt had a sore throat 3 weeks ago. She now has fever 38.4 °C and painful swollen knees, then elbows, each lasting a few days. There is a new apical pansystolic murmur radiating to the axilla. ESR 75 mm/h, CRP 5 mg/dL, ECG PR interval 220 ms.
  1. What is the diagnosis? List the criteria she fulfils. [3]
  2. Which investigations prove a recent streptococcal infection, and which assess the heart? [2]
  3. Outline her acute treatment with doses (eradication + anti-inflammatory). Echo shows moderate MR. [3]
  4. Special: Give the secondary prophylaxis regimen and how long it should continue. [2]
