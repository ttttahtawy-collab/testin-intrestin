Case # Dermatology · 10 marks

A father, his pregnant wife (24 weeks) and their 6-week-old baby all have severe itching that is worse at night. The father has excoriated papules and S-shaped burrows in the finger webs and on the wrists, around the umbilicus and on the genitalia. The baby has lesions on the scalp, palms and soles.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Give 2 features that support it. [2]
  2. Write the father's treatment with application instructions. [3]
  3. Special: Which treatment is safe for the pregnant mother and for the 6-week-old baby? Which drug is contraindicated in both? [3]
  4. What general measures stop re-infestation? [2]

QModel answerMarks
1Scabies (Sarcoptes scabiei). Any 2: nocturnal itch · several family members affected · burrows (S-shaped, ending in a vesicle) · typical sites (finger webs, wrists, axillae, umbilicus, genitalia). The head is spared in adults but involved in infants.1 + 1
2Permethrin 5% cream from neck to toes, left on for 8–12 h (overnight) then washed off; repeat on day 8. Alternatives: Benzyl benzoate 25%, Ivermectin 200 µg/kg orally repeated on day 8. Antihistamine for itch, which can last 2–4 weeks after cure.1 + 1 + 1
3Precipitated sulfur (10% adult / 5% child) nightly for 3–5 days is safe in pregnancy and infants <2 months; Permethrin 2.5% for older infants (include scalp and face in infants).
Ivermectin is contraindicated in pregnancy and in children <15 kg.		1 + 1 + 1
4Treat all close contacts at the same time, even without symptoms. Wash clothes and bed linen at >60 °C, or seal them in a bag for 10 days.1 + 1
Pearl: Crusted (Norwegian) scabies occurs in immunocompromised patients and is highly contagious.
