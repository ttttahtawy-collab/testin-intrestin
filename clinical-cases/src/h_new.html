A 14-year-old boy with sickle cell disease presents with severe pain in his back and legs after a febrile illness and poor fluid intake. Temperature 38.3 °C, mild jaundice, SpO₂ 97%. Hb 7.5 g/dL (baseline 8), reticulocytes 12%.
  1. What is the type of crisis and what precipitated it? [1]
  2. Explain the molecular defect and name 2 smear findings. [2]
  3. Write the acute management. [2]
  4. The next day he has chest pain, cough, SpO₂ 88% and a new infiltrate. What has happened, and what is the key treatment? [2]
  5. Special: List his long-term preventive measures, including how hydroxyurea works. Which crisis causes a sudden fall in Hb with low reticulocytes? [3]

Case # Hematology · 10 marks

An 8-year-old boy has a painful, hot, swollen right knee the day after a minor fall. He has had several similar episodes and a large thigh hematoma after a vaccination. His maternal uncle had a "bleeding problem". Platelets 260 ×10⁹/L, PT normal, aPTT 72 s (control 32 s), bleeding time normal.
  1. What is the most likely diagnosis and its inheritance? [2]
  2. Explain the bleeding pattern (primary vs secondary hemostasis). [1]
  3. What does a 1:1 mixing study show here, and what would non-correction mean? Which test confirms the diagnosis? [3]
  4. How do you treat this bleed? [2]
  5. Special: How does von Willebrand disease differ in the lab? When can DDAVP be used? [2]

QModel answerMarks
1Hemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency) (hemophilia B = factor IX); X-linked recessive.1 + 1
2Secondary (coagulation) defect: hemarthroses, deep muscle hematomas, delayed bleeding (primary/platelet defects give petechiae, mucosal and immediate bleeding).1
3Prolonged aPTT that corrects on mixing = factor deficiency. No correction = inhibitor: immediate = lupus anticoagulant; after 2 h at 37 °C = factor VIII inhibitor (acquired hemophilia). Confirm with a factor VIII assay.1 + 1 + 1
4Recombinant factor VIII concentrate (on demand, then prophylaxis); tranexamic acid / EACA as adjunct; cryoprecipitate or FFP only if concentrate unavailable. Rest, ice, elevation; no IM injections or NSAIDs.1.5 + 0.5
5vWD: autosomal dominant; ↑ bleeding time, variable ↑aPTT, ↓vWF antigen and ristocetin cofactor; mucosal bleeding. DDAVP 0.3 µg/kg: mild hemophilia A and vWD (not hemophilia B).1 + 1
