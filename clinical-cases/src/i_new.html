Case # Immunology · 10 marks

A 22-year-old woman has recurrent episodes of non-itchy swelling of the lips, hands and once the larynx, each lasting 2–3 days, often after dental work or around her periods. Some attacks cause severe abdominal colic; she once had an unnecessary laparotomy. Antihistamines, steroids and epinephrine did not help. Her father has similar attacks.
  1. What is the most likely diagnosis and its inheritance? Which mediator causes the swelling? [2]
  2. Give 3 features that separate it from mast-cell (allergic) angioedema. [1.5]
  3. What is the screening test, and how do you tell type 1 from type 2? [2.5]
  4. Write the treatment of an acute laryngeal attack, and 2 options for prophylaxis. [2.5]
  5. Special: A 60-year-old hypertensive man develops tongue swelling without urticaria. Which drug is the likely cause, and what do you do? [1.5]

2Any 3: no urticaria or itch (tension, burning) · severe abdominal colic mimicking a surgical abdomen · triggers are trauma/dental work/stress/menses · no response to epinephrine, steroids or antihistamines.0.5 each

A 35-year-old woman has had itchy wheals almost every day for 3 months, sometimes with lip swelling. Each wheal disappears within a few hours and leaves no mark. No clear trigger. She also has Hashimoto thyroiditis. Examination between attacks is normal.
  1. What is the diagnosis, and what defines "chronic"? Give the 2 main subtypes. [2]
  2. What investigations are needed? [1]
  3. Outline the stepwise treatment ladder (EAACI/GA²LEN) with time frames. [4]

  5. What is the emergency treatment if she develops airway swelling? [1]

Case # Immunology · 10 marks

A 3-year-old boy has had 4 pneumonias, recurrent otitis media and 2 episodes of sinusitis since the age of 7 months, each needing IV antibiotics. He is failing to thrive. Tonsils are barely visible and no lymph nodes are palpable. His maternal uncle died of infection in childhood. Serum IgG, IgA and IgM are all very low; CD19+ B cells are absent.
  1. What is the most likely diagnosis? Why did infections start after 6 months of age? [2]
  2. List 4 Jeffrey Modell warning signs present or relevant here. [2]
  3. What is the main treatment? [2]
  4. Special: Which vaccines are contraindicated in cellular/combined immunodeficiency? Are inactivated vaccines safe? [2]
  5. Match: (a) hypocalcemia + cardiac defect + absent thymus; (b) eczema + thrombocytopenia + infections; (c) recurrent Neisseria infections; (d) anaphylaxis to blood products. [2]

2Any 4: ≥8 otitis/yr · ≥2 sinusitis · ≥2 pneumonias · need for IV antibiotics · failure to thrive · family history · deep abscesses · ≥2 months of oral antibiotics with little effect.0.5 each
3Lifelong IVIG or SCIG replacement, with prompt antibiotics (± prophylactic co-trimoxazole).2
4Live vaccines: BCG, OPV, MMR, varicella, rotavirus, yellow fever, intranasal flu. Inactivated vaccines are safe.1 + 1
5(a) DiGeorge (22q11) · (b) Wiskott-Aldrich · (c) C5–C9 (terminal complement) deficiency · (d) selective IgA deficiency.0.5 each
