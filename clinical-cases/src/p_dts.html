Case # Psychiatry · 10 marks

On day 3 after a hernia repair, a 48-year-old man becomes acutely confused and agitated, sees insects on the walls, and has a coarse tremor, sweating, temperature 38.2 °C, pulse 124/min and BP 165/100 mmHg. His wife says he drinks a bottle of spirits a day. He had a brief fit yesterday.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Explain how the timeline fits. [2]
  2. What features show this is delirium rather than dementia? Name 3 investigations. [2]
  3. Write the management with doses. Special: what must be given before any glucose, and why? [4]
  4. Name the triad of Wernicke encephalopathy and 2 drugs for relapse prevention. [2]

1Alcohol withdrawal → delirium tremens (mortality 10–20%). Timeline from the last drink: 6–12 h tremor/anxiety/sweating → 12–48 h seizures → 72–96 h DTs (clouded consciousness, vivid visual hallucinations, autonomic hyperactivity).1 + 1
2Delirium: acute onset over hours, fluctuating, clouded consciousness / impaired attention, worse at night (dementia: insidious, clear consciousness until late).
Tests (any 3): glucose, electrolytes incl. Mg and phosphate, LFT, CBC, infection screen, CT brain if head injury or focal signs.		1 + 1
3
  • Thiamine 100 mg parenteral bid ×3–5 days, BEFORE any glucose: glucose without thiamine can precipitate Wernicke encephalopathy. Then oral thiamine for ≥6 months.
  • Benzodiazepine: Chlordiazepoxide 50–100 mg PO q4h or Lorazepam/Diazepam IV (BZDs are the treatment of choice here).
  • IV fluids, correct electrolytes (K, Mg), quiet lit room, reorientation, no restraints.
