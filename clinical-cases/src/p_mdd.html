Case # Psychiatry · 10 marks

A 62-year-old man, widowed 6 months ago, has had 2 months of low mood, loss of interest, early-morning waking, 7 kg weight loss, poor concentration and guilt. He says his children "would be better off without me" and admits he has been saving his sleeping tablets. He has no history of mania. He had an MI last year and takes a β-blocker.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Justify it using DSM-5 criteria. [2]
  2. Which medical and drug causes should be excluded, and with which tests? [2]
  3. Special: List his suicide risk factors and the immediate safety steps. [3]
  4. Give the drug treatment with dose, and state when ECT is indicated. [3]

1Major depressive disorder (severe) with suicidal ideation. ≥5 of 9 symptoms for ≥2 weeks, including depressed mood or anhedonia: mood, anhedonia, sleep, weight, concentration, guilt, suicidal thoughts. Duration and severity exceed normal grief.1 + 1

3Risk factors (any 4): male, elderly, widowed, severe depression, hopelessness, a plan with stockpiled pills, chronic illness.
