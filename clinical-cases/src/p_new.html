Case # Psychiatry · 10 marks

A 22-year-old man has withdrawn from university over the past 8 months. He hears two voices commenting on his actions and believes his neighbours are controlling his thoughts through the TV. His speech is hard to follow and his affect is blunted. He neglects hygiene. He denies drug use; urine drug screen is negative.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Justify it with DSM-5 criteria (symptoms + duration). [2]
  2. Name his positive and negative symptoms. [1]
  3. List 3 differentials to exclude and the tests used. [2]
  4. Give first-line drug treatment with dose and the baseline tests before starting. [2]
  5. Special: On day 5 of haloperidol he is febrile (40 °C), rigid, sweaty, confused, with labile BP and CPK 8000. What is it, and how is it managed? [3]

1Schizophrenia. Criterion A: ≥2 symptoms for ≥1 month (at least one of delusions, hallucinations, disorganised speech) — he has hallucinations, delusions, disorganised speech, negative symptoms; B: marked functional decline; C: continuous disturbance ≥6 months.1 + 1
2Positive: auditory hallucinations, delusions of control/persecution, disorganised speech. Negative: blunted affect, withdrawal, avolition/self-neglect.0.5 + 0.5

Case # Psychiatry · 10 marks

A 27-year-old man has slept 2 hours a night for 10 days but feels full of energy. He talks rapidly, jumps between topics, says he will become president, and has spent all his savings on cars. He had a depressive episode 2 years ago. He is irritable when interrupted.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Justify it (duration + 3 features). [2]
  2. How does bipolar II differ? [1]
  3. Outline acute and maintenance treatment. When is prophylaxis indicated? [3]
  4. Special: Months later on lithium he develops vomiting, diarrhea, coarse tremor, ataxia and slurred speech after starting a diuretic. What is it, and how is it managed? [3]
  5. Why should antidepressants be avoided alone in his future depressive episodes? [1]

Case # — Answers & marking scheme

QModel answerMarks
1Bipolar I disorder, current episode mania: elevated/irritable mood ≥1 week (any duration if hospitalised) + ≥3 of: ↓need for sleep, pressured speech, flight of ideas, grandiosity, reckless spending, distractibility, ↑activity.1 + 1
2Bipolar II: hypomania (≥4 days, no marked impairment/psychosis) + major depression; never full mania.1
3Acute: antipsychotic (olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine) for rapid control ± lithium (first-line, 80% response, 1–2 week lag) or valproate/carbamazepine. Maintenance: lithium/mood stabiliser + psychoeducation, social-rhythm therapy. Prophylaxis if ≥2 episodes in 5 years, or 1 severe episode + family history.1 + 1 + 1
4Lithium toxicity (thiazides ↓ lithium clearance). Stop lithium, check level, IV saline ± sodium bicarbonate; hemodialysis is definitive (severe toxicity, encephalopathy, convulsions, renal failure).1 + 2
5Antidepressant monotherapy can switch him into mania / rapid cycling; use with a mood stabiliser (or quetiapine).1

Case # Psychiatry · 10 marks

A 26-year-old woman has visited the ED 4 times in 2 months with sudden attacks of palpitations, chest tightness, breathlessness, tingling fingers, dizziness and a feeling she is about to die. Each attack peaks within 10 minutes and settles within 30. ECGs and troponins were normal. She now avoids going out alone in case an attack happens.
  1. What is the diagnosis? Give the DSM-5 criteria. What co-existing problem is suggested by her avoidance? [2]
  2. List 4 medical conditions or substances that can mimic panic attacks, and a test for one. [2]
  3. How do you manage an acute attack and the long-term condition? [3]
  4. Special: How does generalised anxiety disorder differ? What is the difference between anxiety and phobia? [2]
  5. Which risk must be assessed in every patient with panic disorder? [1]

Case # — Answers & marking scheme

QModel answerMarks
1Panic disorder: recurrent unexpected attacks peaking within minutes + ≥1 month of worry about attacks or behaviour change. Avoidance suggests agoraphobia.1 + 1
2Any 4: pheochromocytoma (24-h urine catecholamines/metanephrines) · hyperthyroidism (TSH) · hypoparathyroidism · B12 deficiency · cardiac arrhythmia/ischemia · caffeine, cocaine, amphetamines, bronchodilators · alcohol/BZD withdrawal.1.5 + 0.5
3Acute: reassurance, breathing control, a short-acting benzodiazepine only if needed. Long-term: SSRI/SNRI (start low, as SSRI start can worsen anxiety) + CBT with interoceptive exposure; avoid long-term BZDs.1 + 2
4GAD: excessive, uncontrollable, constant worry about many things for ≥6 months + tension, restlessness, fatigue, poor sleep (not discrete attacks). Anxiety = diffuse apprehension with a vague threat; phobia = fear of a specific known object or situation.1 + 1
5Suicide risk.1

A 16-year-old girl is brought by her mother after losing 12 kg in 6 months; BMI is now 15. She exercises for hours, eats very little and says she "still has a fat stomach". Her periods stopped 4 months ago. Pulse 46/min, BP 85/55 mmHg, temperature 35.6 °C, fine downy hair on her arms.
  1. What is the diagnosis? State the core criteria and the 2 subtypes. [2]
  2. List 4 physical or lab complications. [2]
  3. Special: She is admitted for feeding. On day 3 she develops arrhythmia and heart failure. What is this, which electrolyte is key, and how is it prevented? [3]
  4. Outline long-term management. [1]
  5. How does bulimia nervosa differ (2 clinical signs + typical acid-base change), and which drug is approved for it? [2]
